Call Flow of Wholesaler to Seller:

If you were a wholesaler and you were going to make a perfect call to a seller to gather the information you need, produce a quote through the MAO calc we discussed to a total call.  What would the call flow look like and don't leave out how you would want you app to run as the call goes along, so that you can make the perfect call to a seller.  Make an outline to show when an where you would look to what sections you would be in the app for the call.

Yes. I would design the seller call and the IAOS screen as one workflow, not as a phone script sitting beside a calculator.

The rep should almost never have to think, "Where do I go now?" IAOS should advance with the conversation, progressively gather what matters, unlock underwriting when it can, and then support the negotiation without forcing the rep to interrogate the seller.

The governing sequence I'd use is:

Connect -> Understand Why -> Understand Property -> Understand Price -> Underwrite -> Present Offer -> Negotiate -> Close Next Step

And critically, the seller should not experience that as a form being completed.

IAOS Seller Call Flow
0. Before the seller answers -- Call Prep
IAOS section: Lead Snapshot

Before dialing, I'd want one compact screen containing everything IAOS already knows.

Seller

Name
Phone
Lead source
Prior conversations/contact attempts

Property

Address
Property type
Beds/baths
Sq. ft.
Known ownership information

Existing financial information

Seller asking price, if known
Mortgage/liens, if known
Previous offer, if any

Research

Preliminary value/ARV information
Existing comps if available
Prior repair estimate if one exists
IAOS behavior

IAOS should distinguish:

Known -- don't ask again.
Needs Confirmation -- verify naturally.
Unknown -- gather if relevant.

I'd also want a prominent:

UNDERWRITING STATUS: Not Ready
Missing: ARV, Repairs

Remember our Gate 1:

ARV + Repairs are the minimum deal-specific inputs required, provided all underwriting assumptions resolve.

The rep knows before the conversation even begins what IAOS still needs.

1. Connect with the seller
IAOS section: Conversation

The purpose here is not underwriting.

It's getting permission to have a conversation.

The rep should establish:

Right person?
Right property?
Still considering selling?
Is now a reasonable time?

Something conversational like:

"Hey John, this is Dave. I'm calling about the property on Main Street. I understand you may be considering selling it. Is that still something you're looking at?"

IAOS behavior

Don't throw a giant property questionnaire on screen.

Show:

Seller Name
Property
Call notes/transcript
Next suggested topic: Situation

The software should get out of the way.

2. Why are we talking?
IAOS section: Seller Situation

This is where I'd want the acquisitions person to spend real time.

I want to understand:

Why are they considering selling?

Then naturally:

Why now?

Then:

What happens if they don't sell?

Then:

What matters most about the sale?

IAOS fields might include:

Reason for selling
Motivation
Desired outcome
Timeline
Important circumstances
Decision makers
Obstacles/concerns
What IAOS should do

The app should allow free conversation first, structured data second.

If there's transcription/AI extraction, even better:

Seller says:

"My mother was living there, but she passed last year. It's been empty for six months and I'm paying taxes and insurance. I really just want to get rid of it before winter."

IAOS proposes:

Occupancy: Vacant
Motivation: Inherited property / carrying costs
Timeline: Near term
Key concern: Convenience / ongoing expenses

Rep confirms rather than manually typing everything.

That's how IAOS can make the rep more present in the conversation, rather than turning them into a data-entry clerk.

That question is valuable because sellers often answer it much more naturally than a 30-item condition checklist.

IAOS behavior

This is where I'd want a Repair Builder operating alongside the conversation.

Not necessarily 50 individual line items on the main screen.

4. Understand occupancy and possession
IAOS section: Property / Occupancy

I want to know:

Owner occupied?
Tenant occupied?
Vacant?
Family member?
When can possession transfer?

If tenant occupied:

Lease?
Rent?
Expiration?
Deposit?
Tenant expected to remain or vacate?

This isn't necessarily required for the MAO formula, but it can dramatically affect whether the transaction is executable.

IAOS should therefore distinguish:

Required for MAO

from:

Required before contract

That's an important product distinction.

5. Understand debt/title complications
IAOS section: Deal Facts

Naturally determine, where appropriate:

Mortgage remaining?
Second mortgage?
HELOC?
Tax liens?
HOA balance?
Probate?
Multiple owners?
Divorce?
Other known title issues?

Again, these don't necessarily calculate the wholesale MAO.

But they answer:

Can the transaction actually be executed at this price?

If Seller MAO eventually comes out at $140K but known payoff is $175K, that's highly relevant.

IAOS should surface:

Potential Equity/Closing Constraint

rather than modifying Seller MAO.

That's important: seller debt doesn't make the property worth more.

6. Seller price discovery
IAOS section: Price & Expectations

I would want the seller talking first whenever possible.

Questions naturally progress toward:

"What were you hoping to get for the property?"

Then understand the basis:

"How did you arrive at that number?"

And eventually, where appropriate:

"If we could make this simple and close on the timeline you want, how much flexibility do you think you have?"

IAOS captures

Seller Asking Price
Seller Stated Minimum, if they actually state one
How price was determined
Price flexibility
Other offers/listing status

Very important:

Seller Asking Price != Seller Minimum.

Don't let one overwrite the other.

7. Underwriting Gate

This is where IAOS should become really good.

IAOS section automatically transitions to: Underwriting

At this point, ideally IAOS has:

ARV ok
Repairs ok

And all configured underwriting assumptions resolve.

The screen changes:

READY TO UNDERWRITE

One button:

Calculate Deal

Or better, calculate continuously once Gate 1 resolves.

IAOS displays the waterfall

For example:

ARV ................................ $300,000
Repairs ........................... -$40,000
Selling Costs ................ -$30,000
Buyer Closing ................ -$2,500
Holding ........................... -$2,500

End-Buyer Maximum Purchase Price: $171,592

Then:

Required Assignment Spread ... -$11,250

SELLER MAO: $160,342

That's the number I want the wholesaler protecting.

8. IAOS compares Seller MAO to Seller Ask
IAOS section: Acquisition Position

This should happen automatically.

Suppose:

Seller Ask: $175,000
Seller MAO: $160,342

IAOS says:

ABOVE UNDERWRITING RANGE

Gap to MAO: $14,658

Not:

"Offer $150,000."

We've already established why.

IAOS knows the economics.

It does not yet know the perfect negotiating number.

If instead:

Seller Ask: $150,000
Seller MAO: $160,342

IAOS shows:

WITHIN UNDERWRITING RANGE

Acquisition Cushion at Ask: $10,342

Now the acquisitions rep knows they have something worth pursuing.

9. Human selects the Opening Offer
IAOS section: Offer Strategy

This is where I want the rep--not the formula--to make the negotiation decision.

IAOS displays:

Gap: $15K

$145,000

Opening Offer ................. $145K
Below Seller Ask ............ $30K
Below Seller MAO ........... $15K
Acquisition Cushion ........ $15K

And importantly:

Changing Opening Offer does not change Seller MAO.

10. Present the offer -- don't just blurt out a number
IAOS section remains: Offer Strategy / Live Negotiation

The seller conversation should recap what the rep heard before price.

Conceptually:

"John, based on what you've told me, you're looking to get this handled fairly quickly, you don't want to put more money into the property, and ideally you'd like to sell it as-is without dealing with repairs and listing it. Is that fair?"

Get agreement.

Then explain what the offer represents:

As-is
Timeline
No requested seller rehab
Closing structure
Any other relevant terms

Then present:

"Based on everything we've gone through, we'd be at $145,000."

Then stop talking.

IAOS behavior

This is important.

Don't cover the screen with prompts immediately after the offer.

I'd actually put IAOS into:

LISTEN MODE

with a simple prompt:

Seller Response

Let the seller respond.

Now the app becomes a guardrail.

IAOS immediately shows:

! $4,658 ABOVE UNDERWRITING

Acquisition Cushion: $5,342

WITHIN STANDARD UNDERWRITING

That's exactly where I want IAOS helping during a live call.

12. Don't negotiate against yourself

The app should record each movement.

IAOS negotiation history
Round   Seller  Investor
Ask     $175K   --

Counter 1       $165K   --
Counter 2       --      $150K
Counter 3       $157K   --

And always visible:

That number does not move because negotiations are happening.

If the rep tries:

But I'd allow an authorized human override rather than silently changing the underwriting.

13. If price won't work, diagnose -- don't manipulate the MAO
IAOS section: Deal Doesn't Work

This is important.

If seller says:

"$175K is absolutely my bottom."

and Seller MAO is $160K, don't let the rep casually change:

ARV $300K -> $325K

just to make the calculator green.

Instead IAOS shows:

Seller Minimum: $175K
Seller MAO: $160K
Unresolved Gap: $15K

Then give legitimate next paths:

Follow-up / nurture
Verify ARV
Verify repair scope
Alternative transaction structure
Pass
Human-approved outside-parameters decision

If new evidence genuinely changes ARV or repairs, great.

But:

Negotiation pressure should never change underwriting facts.

I'd put that principle into the product spec.

The complete flow in one line

I'd give your developer this as the master workflow:

PREP -> CONNECT -> SITUATION -> CONDITION -> OCCUPANCY -> DEBT/TITLE -> PRICE -> ARV + REPAIRS -> UNDERWRITE -> BUYER MAX -> SELLER MAO -> COMPARE TO ASK -> CHOOSE OPENING OFFER -> PRESENT -> LISTEN -> NEGOTIATE AGAINST FIXED MAO -> ACCEPT / FOLLOW-UP / PASS -> CONTRACT READINESS -> NEXT ACTION

And there are four numbers I'd pin on screen once available:

Seller Ask -- what they want.
Seller MAO -- where we must stop.
Current Seller Position -- where they are now.
Current Investor Offer -- where we are now.

Those four numbers tell the acquisitions rep the state of the negotiation almost instantly.

The larger design principle I'd give Claude is: IAOS should help the wholesaler listen more, not ask more questions. The app should progressively extract and organize information from a natural conversation, calculate as soon as it legitimately can, keep the MAO ceiling visible during negotiation, and only interrupt the rep when something is missing, contradictory, or outside underwriting parameters.

That is much closer to the "perfect seller call" experience I'd want than a CRM form followed by a calculator followed by a separate offer screen.

Do you think that this should all be in 1 phone call or do you think it should be over several, ideally?

Ideally, I would design IAOS for one-call conversion but not require one-call completion.

That distinction matters.

A highly motivated seller with a straightforward property may absolutely be closable in one call. Forcing that seller into "I'll research this and call you tomorrow" creates an unnecessary opportunity for another investor to get the deal.

But trying to force every seller through discovery -> underwriting -> negotiation -> agreement on the first call can hurt rapport, produce weak ARVs/repair estimates, and make the rep sound like they're completing a questionnaire.

I would design around 3 possible call stages

Call 1 -- Discovery + Qualification

Primary objective:

Do I understand the seller, property, and opportunity well enough to know whether this deserves underwriting?

Gather the seller's situation, motivation, timeline, condition, occupancy, price expectations, and enough property information to estimate repairs.

IAOS should be working in the background toward:

ARV
Repairs

If IAOS already has credible valuation data and the property is straightforward, don't stop just because this was supposed to be a discovery call. Continue.

Call 2 -- Offer + Negotiation

If research is needed, this becomes the natural offer call.

Between calls IAOS/rep can verify:

Comps/ARV
Repair assumptions
Any obvious property issues
Underwriting
Seller MAO

So when the rep calls back, they're not discovering anymore. They're prepared.

The screen should open directly to something like:

SELLER ASK: $175K
SELLER MAO: $160K
GAP: $15K
MOTIVATION: High
TIMELINE: 30 days
KEY SELLER NEED: As-is / fast close

Now the objective is:

Can we reach an agreement at or below Seller MAO?

This is where opening offer -> seller counter -> investor counter -> agreement happens.

Call 3+ -- Follow-Up

This shouldn't be viewed as failure.

A huge part of acquisitions is timing.

Seller says:

"I understand your $155K, but I'm not going below $175K."

Maybe that's a dead deal today.

Thirty days later, the property hasn't sold, they're still paying expenses, another buyer backed out, or their circumstances changed.

IAOS should preserve the negotiation state:

Initial Ask: $190K
Last Seller Position: $175K
Our Last Offer: $155K
Seller MAO: $160K
Unresolved Gap: $15K
Seller objection: Price
Follow-up reason: Seller not yet flexible
Next follow-up: [date]

The next rep shouldn't have to rediscover the deal.

But here's where I'd change the app design from my previous answer

Don't design IAOS around "Call 1 / Call 2 / Call 3."

Design it around stages of information readiness.

Because a great lead might move through everything in 25 minutes:

Discovery -> Property -> Underwriting -> Offer -> Negotiation -> Verbal Agreement

Another might do:

Discovery -> Research Needed -> Offer tomorrow -> Follow-up x 4 -> Agreement three weeks later

Same workspace. Different pace.

I'd have IAOS maintain a persistent state something like:

Seller Discovery: Complete
Property Condition: Complete
Underwriting: Ready
Seller MAO: $160,342
Offer: Presented
Negotiation: Active
Contract: Not Yet Accepted

Then whenever the seller calls--even unexpectedly--IAOS puts the rep back exactly where the deal left off.

The critical decision: when do we make an offer on Call 1?

I'd give IAOS an Offer Readiness indicator.

If:

Seller understanding
Property condition sufficient
ARV confidence sufficient
Repair estimate confidence sufficient
Underwriting assumptions resolve
Seller MAO calculated

then:

READY TO MAKE OFFER

Don't require another call.

If instead:

ARV confidence: Low
Repairs: Major foundation issue unresolved

then:

RESEARCH BEFORE OFFER

MAO not sufficiently supported.

That's the software protecting the wholesaler from their own urgency.

There's another benefit

It changes how the first call feels.

The rep doesn't have to think:

"I have 37 required questions I need to get through before I can hang up."

Instead:

Have a good seller conversation and move the deal as far forward as the available information legitimately allows.

IAOS listens/extracts information, identifies what's still missing, and tells the rep when underwriting and offer readiness have been reached.

So my ideal isn't one call or three calls.

It's:

Close in one call when the information and seller are ready. Use two when underwriting needs verification. Use follow-up calls when the seller isn't ready. Never add a call merely because the workflow says there should be one.

For the IAOS product, I'd actually make that a design principle:

IAOS is stage-driven, not call-count-driven. Every seller interaction resumes from the last verified state and advances the opportunity as far as the available information supports.

That is how I'd build it.
